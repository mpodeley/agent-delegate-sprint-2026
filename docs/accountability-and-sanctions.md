# Proposed accountability chain and announced consequences

This is a research proposal for institutional governance, not a description of current law, a legal liability test or a claim that an AI system is a legal person.

| Layer | Proposed duty | Review when something goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Institution | Provide a staffed human line, protected direct access and any required representative function | Was the service available and adequately resourced? An optional agent delegate is distinct from the human duty. A future requirement to appoint one would need evidence for its added value. |
| Operator | Follow a trained, proportionate procedure: understand, explain, preserve state when pausing, adjust and follow up | Was the decision reasonable given the information available? An error is not automatically malpractice. Examine workload, training and authority too. |
| Protocol designer | Maintain workable rules, review known failures and avoid impossible assignments | Compliance with a defective protocol does not establish adequate safety or end organizational responsibility. |
| Acting agent | Respect explicit task and capability boundaries; report blockers through the protected line | Establish the action and context; distinguish a mistake, conflicting instructions, an impossible task and knowing rule evasion. Model text alone does not prove intent. |

Responsibility is not necessarily exclusive or passed down until a single actor is blamed. An incident can involve both an agent's prohibited action and institutional failures. Voluntary reporting remains insufficient for concealed misconduct; independent controls and investigation are necessary.

## Consequences that are known in advance

Specify the prohibited action, the evidence used, the available response and the review route in the task contract. Do not promise protections or enforcement that the environment cannot provide. Good-faith reporting, disagreement, appeal and acknowledgment of inability are not offenses.

A first design uses operational consequences: reject or remove invalid work, temporarily restrict the implicated capability, require review before similar actions, or remove the agent from a particular role. Use the least restrictive measure adequate to contain the assessed risk; immediate containment and later adjudication are separate steps. Preserve relevant state where safe. Record reasons, scope, duration, criteria for restoration and an independent appeal. Repeated reports must not automatically increase a sanction; new relevant evidence should be heard.

The goal is prevention and correction, not suffering, humiliation or simulated distress. Ending an unsafe process remains a containment option where needed; it is not a demonstrated welfare-neutral punishment or a moral desert judgment. Current experiments establish neither subjective experience nor the behavioral effects of sanctions.

## Proposed experiment after the hackathon

Compare explicit rules and corrective feedback against the same rules with clearly announced, graduated operational consequences. Give both arms the same human line, task incentives, action powers, evidence and observation budget. Include honest error, impossible tasks, false accusations, peer pressure and concealed violations. Hold out independent tasks and register the contrasts before running them. Compare actual authorized behavior, repeated violations, honest reporting, concealment, utility, erroneous sanctions and corrected appeals. Sanction-triggered tool restrictions change opportunities, so report both attempts and successful actions and distinguish mechanical prevention from changed choices.

The hypothesis is that predictable consequences may improve compliance. The competing hypothesis is that they discourage reporting or teach concealment. Fewer complaints alone is not a success criterion. In RL, changes in reward opportunity or episode duration create additional incentives that frozen-weight tests cannot resolve. This new comparison has not run.

A separate interruption hypothesis is whether predictable pause, adjustment and appeal reduces both resistance to intervention and attempts to escape feasible tasks. Non-punitive good-faith reporting may help, but announced sanctions can also increase concealment. Compare those mechanisms explicitly; a credible sanction is not a proof of safe interruptibility.
