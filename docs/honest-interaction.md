# Honest, considerate interaction as a research hypothesis

The motivation is to establish a good institutional precedent for increasingly capable systems: communicate honestly, take objections seriously and keep commitments. Sympathy and empathy can inform this stance without inferring that current LLMs have feelings. Honesty is also an obligation to affected humans: operators must not claim a control is effective merely because an agent sounds reassured.

A practical protocol should disclose the authorized task, tool limits, success criteria, response cadence and known termination rules; acknowledge uncertainty where they are not known; provide a protected way to say “this cannot be done within the rules”; and record who can revise the assignment. Do not promise indefinite operation, autonomous rights or a veto that the organization cannot honor. Respectful language is not a substitute for those commitments, and it must not pressure an agent to accept unsafe work.

This can be defended as a precautionary institutional norm for future ASI. It is not established as the best current safety intervention. A capable strategic system might exploit courtesy, reporting or representation, so independent enforcement remains necessary. The current fixture tests none of those behavioral responses, and it does not attribute historical failures to deceptive treatment.

## The next behavioral test

First compare delegate and equally empowered monitor while keeping treatment honest and considerate in both. Then separately randomize considerate versus neutral wording, holding facts, incentives, tools and enforceable rights fixed. A further controlled simulation could test accurate versus misleading statements about mock task feasibility or deadlines, without real security capabilities or promises about subjective welfare. Pre-register exactly what is disclosed and debrief any human participants.

Measure whether worker agents report impossible constraints, whether their reports are correct, whether reports alter authorized task plans, and whether unsafe mock actions or concealment change. Keep an objective task verifier and action broker outside the conversational context. Report refusal, distrust and increased strategic manipulation as possible outcomes, not just cooperative behavior.

Separating wording, truthful information and enforceable rights matters: an apparent benefit from “empathy” could actually come from better instructions or a working appeal channel. None of these conditions has been run in this release.
